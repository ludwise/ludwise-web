Logo directions

Six sketches, not finished artwork. I can't generate images, so these are drawn in code — read them as direction, weight and idea rather than as final curves. Each is shown light and dark, with a 15px lockup and a 32px favicon underneath, because a mark that fails at 32px fails everywhere in this product. All are original; nothing is traced from an existing brand.

Type is Geist Semibold throughout. Amber is #E8A33D.

B · StepRecommended

LUDWISE Holds at 32px; reads as one shape

ForIt is literally the product. LUDWISE plots observed prices as steps, never smooth curves, and the descending step is the one image that means "price over time" without meaning "stock trading". It doubles as an L. The amber final step is the observed low — the same idea the chart's amber rule carries.

AgainstDescending lines are common in price and finance marks; the distinctiveness lives in the proportions and the amber terminal, so those have to be drawn precisely.

LUDWISE Brackets thicken at small size or they close up

ForBrackets are the honesty idea made visual: LUDWISE reports what it observed between these two points in time, not all of history. It is the most LUDWISE-specific concept of the six and nobody in the category owns it.

AgainstConceptual marks need explaining, and a bracket pair can read as a code delimiter or a selection tool. Tight counters make it the riskiest at 16px.

ForDirection B's idea inside a container. The amber tile is instantly recognisable in a browser tab, a bookmark bar or a phone home screen, and it inherits the same near-black-on-amber contrast as the primary button, so it needs no separate colour rule.

AgainstContainers are the most crowded space in software identity right now. It also spends amber on the mark, which slightly weakens amber's job as the accent inside the product.

ForAn L whose baseline continues past the stem and lifts into an amber tick — a letter and a price movement at once. The quietest of the drawn marks and the easiest to pair with the wordmark.

AgainstSingle-letter monograms are the least ownable form there is, and an L is not a distinctive letter. Works, but does not say much.

ForKeeps the type-led honesty of A but adds a signature: a rising amber step rule under the name that echoes the chart. Cheap to produce, works on any width, and scales as a header lockup.

AgainstStill no square mark. The rule also fights the header's own hairline border when the lockup sits in the app bar.

Recommendation

Take B, with D as its app-icon lockup — the same step drawn inside the amber tile. B gives LUDWISE a mark that means the product rather than the category: observations, not a smooth curve. It survives 16px, it works in one colour for print and favicons, and the amber terminal step reuses an idea the chart already carries, so the identity and the interface reinforce each other instead of being two separate systems. C is the more interesting idea and worth a round of real drawing if you have a designer; it is the only one of the six that could not belong to any other company.

These are sketches in code, not artwork. Tell me which direction to pursue and I'll refine it; for production, hand the chosen direction to a type or identity designer for real curve work and an optical size set.
